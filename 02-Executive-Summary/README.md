# Executive Summary

## African National Leadership and Accountability Model (ANLAM)

**A Proposed Framework for Presidential Leadership Assessment, Accountability and National Development**

**Pilot Case: Nigeria**

**Proponent:** Ekundayo Folorunso

**Status:** Independent policy proposal submitted for public discussion, expert review and possible institutional consideration.

### 1. Background

Africa's development challenges require more than economic resources and development programmes. They also require accountable leadership, strong institutions, policy continuity and a clear national development direction.

Nigeria, as one of Africa's largest countries, provides an important case study for examining how presidential leadership can be assessed and held accountable throughout an administration.

### 2. The Proposal

ANLAM proposes the establishment of a 36-member Board of National Integrity and Presidential Accountability (BNIPA), with one representative from each of Nigeria's 36 states.

The proposed Board would operate as an independent presidential accountability and leadership assessment institution, subject to constitutional safeguards.

Its responsibilities would include:

- Assessing national development priorities.
- Identifying the leadership competencies required to address national challenges.
- Publishing independent assessments of presidential candidates and their proposed leadership capacity.
- Monitoring the elected President's performance against declared national commitments and manifesto priorities.
- Publishing periodic performance reports for public scrutiny.

### 3. Institutional Safeguards

The proposed framework includes:

- One representative from each state.
- A minimum recognised university bachelor's degree or equivalent qualification.
- Transparent selection and public scrutiny.
- One non-renewable eight-year term, subject to transitional arrangements.
- Protection against arbitrary presidential removal.
- No personal salaries, stipends or allowances for Board members.
- Transparent funding for legitimate institutional operations and independent research.

The Board would not replace the President, National Assembly, judiciary, electoral institutions, state governments or local governments.

### 4. Democratic Accountability

ANLAM is not intended to establish a parallel government.

The President would retain constitutional authority and responsibility for governing, including appointing members of the cabinet under existing law.

Citizens would retain their democratic right to elect the President. The Board would not declare election results or exercise the powers of the electoral commission.

Any proposal affecting ballot access or constitutional electoral arrangements would require independent constitutional examination.

### 5. Africa's Development Future

ANLAM also considers the relationship between accountable leadership and Africa's long-term development.

Its broader ambition is to encourage conditions that enable skilled Africans to remain, return, invest, innovate and contribute to national development.

The proposal recognises that reducing brain drain requires improvements in employment, education, healthcare, infrastructure, industrialisation, technology, security and institutional quality.

### 6. Invitation to National Debate

ANLAM is an independent proposal, not an adopted policy of the Nigerian Government or African Union.

It is presented for constructive public debate, constitutional review, expert assessment and possible improvement.

The proponent welcomes engagement with policymakers, constitutional lawyers, political scientists, governance researchers, civil society organisations, journalists and interested members of the public.

**Our guiding vision:**

*We must build an Africa where our brightest minds do not have to leave to become their best.*
